## Key takeaways

- **MRR** shows the size and direction of the business.
- **Churn** shows whether customers stay.
- **Gross margin** shows whether each sale is worth making.
- **CAC payback** shows how long it takes to earn back the cost of winning a customer.
- **Runway** shows how long you can keep going.

</div>

## Why a finance view matters

SaaS dashboards are full of numbers. A finance person narrows them down to the few that predict whether the business will still be standing in two years. These five are where I start with any SaaS product.

## 1. Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)

**MRR** is the total subscription revenue you can expect every month, with annual plans divided by 12.

Track it in four parts, not just one total:

- **New MRR** from new customers;
- **Expansion MRR** from upgrades;
- **Contraction MRR** from downgrades;
- **Churned MRR** from cancellations.

A flat total can hide strong sales and heavy cancellations cancelling each other out.

## 2. Churn

**Customer churn** = customers lost in the month ÷ customers at the start of the month.

**Revenue churn** does the same with MRR. Revenue churn matters more, because losing one large customer hurts more than losing several small ones. If expansion from existing customers exceeds churn, you have **net negative churn**, which is the healthiest sign a SaaS business can show.

## 3. Gross margin

**Gross margin** = (revenue − cost of delivering the service) ÷ revenue.

Cost of delivery includes hosting, third-party APIs (including AI model usage), payment fees and customer support. AI features can quietly push this cost up, so measure it per customer and per plan.

## 4. CAC payback

**Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** = sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers won.

**CAC payback (months)** = CAC ÷ (monthly revenue per customer × gross margin).

The shorter the payback, the faster growth pays for itself. A long payback means every new customer drains cash for months before contributing.

## 5. Cash runway

**Runway (months)** = cash in the bank ÷ average monthly net burn.

Use the burn after collections, not the profit and loss figure. Annual plans paid upfront improve cash even when MRR is unchanged — which is one reason to offer them.

## Putting it together

Review these five every month on one page. When one moves the wrong way, it usually tells you where to look next: churn points to product or onboarding, margin to hosting and API costs, payback to sales and marketing.
